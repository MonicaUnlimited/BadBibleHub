Claims God Knows Everything, But God Asks Questions and Runs Tests1 John 3:20 vs Mark 12:6, Genesis 18:21God Not Omniscient

Scripture excerpt

1 John 3:20 - “God is greater than our hearts, and he knows everything.” vs Genesis 18:21 - “I will go down to see if what they have done is as bad as the outcry... If not, I will know.”

Reference: 1 John 3:20 vs Mark 12:6, Genesis 18:21 (New Testament)

Critical commentary

1 John asserts God is omniscient, yet the Old Testament repeatedly depicts God conducting physical site inspections because He doesn't know what's happening.

Common Christian apologetics

  1. Anthropomorphic Expression: Scripture uses human-relatable language ('God coming down', 'testing') to communicate divine interactions to human minds.
  2. Preserving Human Agency: God conducts tests or asks questions to allow humans to exercise genuine free choice and demonstrate faith.
  3. Rhetorical Pedagogy: Questions from God (like 'Where are you?') are designed to prompt human reflection rather than gather missing information.

Humanist rebuttals

  1. Explicit statements like 'Now I know that you fear God' (Gen 22:12) or God regretting actions (Gen 6:6) describe literal divine ignorance.
  2. Testing humans to 'discover' what is in their hearts directly contradicts the concept of an all-knowing entity who foresees all events.
  3. Dismissing literal statements of divine limitation as mere 'metaphor' is an unprovable, circular defense to excuse biblical errors.

Scripture excerpt

“The Lord was with the men of Judah. They took possession of the hill country, but they could not drive the people from the plains, because they had chariots fitted with iron.”

Reference: Judges 1:19 (Old Testament)

Critical commentary

Common Christian apologetics

  1. Israel's Failure of Faith: Judah was halted by iron chariots because of their own spiritual cowardice and lack of trust, not God's inability.

Humanist rebuttals

  1. The text explicitly states 'The Lord was with Judah', yet they 'could not drive them out because they had iron chariots' (Judges 1:19).
  2. Attributing military failure directly to iron weaponry exposes a clear limit on divine power within the ancient narrative.
  3. Claiming God needed iron chariots to 'train' Israel is an after-the-fact theological excuse for a clear battlefield defeat.

Scripture excerpt

“Your male and female slaves are to come from the nations around you; from them you may buy slaves... You can bequeath them to your children as inherited property and can make them slaves for life.”

Reference: Leviticus 25:44-46 (Old Testament)

Critical commentary

Not only does God allow human ownership, but humans are explicitly categorized as inherited family property right alongside livestock.

Common Christian apologetics

  1. Cultural Accommodation: God regulated and moderated existing brutal ancient practices rather than causing economic collapse by sudden abolition.

  2. An omnibenevolent God who strictly prohibited eating shellfish or working on Saturdays could easily have issued a command forbidding human ownership.
  3. Commanding slaves to submit to harsh masters 'with fear and trembling' (1 Peter 2:18, Ephesians 6:5) was used for centuries to defend human trafficking.
